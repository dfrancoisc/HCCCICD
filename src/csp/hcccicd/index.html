How this works

Six steps from opening the Interoperability page to your change running in Production. Read it once; you will not need it again.

1

Open Change Control and start a change

In the Interoperability page, click Change Control in the toolbar. Go to My change and fill in two things: your ticket reference, and one line saying what you are about to do. Click Start working.

Why it matters This is the only step you have to remember. It gives you a private copy of the interface configuration, so nothing you do is visible to anyone else — or breaks anything for anyone else — until you choose to send it forward.

Did you already start building? That is fine and it is recoverable. Nothing is lost — go to My change and the tool will show you the work it found and offer to put it into a change for you. Read step 6 for the details.
2

Close this and go build, exactly as you do today

Nothing about how you work changes. Use the Interoperability editor, the Management Portal, the CSV record wizard, the rule editor, the transformation builder — or just ask the Agentic Integration Builder to build it for you.

What happens on its own Every time you press Save, whatever you saved is captured and given a version number. There is no export step, no "add to source control" button, and nothing to remember. A brand new namespace, production, transformation or lookup table is captured as version 1 the moment it exists.

3

Check what was captured

Come back to Change Control and open What I changed. Everything you touched is listed, grouped by kind, each with its version, when it changed, and which tool made the change.

Worth knowing This is broader than code. Namespaces, productions, business hosts, adapters, transformations, business processes, routing rules, lookup tables, message schemas, record maps, system default settings, OAuth clients, credentials and globals are all covered. If you changed it, it is here.

4

Send it forward

Send forward is four steps. Describe what you did and why — the approver reads this. Tick the items that belong to this change. Let the safety check run. Review and submit.

The safety check is the point It follows every reference out of everything you ticked and compares it with what is actually running in the target environment. If you are sending a production but not the business host it points at, it says so, and adds it for you in one click. It also tells you which settings have no value in the target, and which passwords will not travel. Anything that would break is fixed here, not discovered there.

5

Watch it land, then send it on

My requests shows where every change you have sent is: deployed, waiting for approval, or sent back to you with a reason. Development deploys itself. Test needs one approval. Production needs two and goes in during a change window.

Moving to the next environment Test it where it landed. When you are happy, press Send on to … on the request. The same items go forward — you do not pick them again — and the safety check runs again against the new target, because Production is not Test.

6

If you built something before starting a change

Very common, and nothing is lost. Capture does not depend on you remembering step 1 — every save is captured regardless. What you are missing is the label that says which piece of work it belongs to, and the hold that stops someone else editing the same thing.

Open My change. Anything captured that is not part of a change appears at the top under Work not in a change yet. Tick what belongs together, give it a reference and a description, and press Put this into a change. From that moment it behaves as though you had started properly, and you can send it forward normally.

The one real risk. While your work was unassigned, nothing was holding those items for you. If somebody else edited the same item in the meantime, one of you has overwritten the other. The tool checks for exactly that when you adopt the work and tells you which items are affected before you commit to anything. Starting the change first is what avoids the situation entirely.

What this does behind the scenes

Your saves were exported to the repository working tree all along — capture never depended on a branch existing. Adopting creates the feature branch now and carries the uncommitted working-tree changes onto it, then registers the edit claims retroactively. The collision check compares each item against the base branch to see whether anyone else committed a change to the same artifact while yours was unassigned.

Start a change

Give this piece of work a name and a reference. You get your own private copy of the interface configuration, and the items you touch are held for you so nobody overwrites them.

What this does behind the scenes

Creates a feature branch interface/<user>/<reference> from the live branch in the Health Connect Cloud GitLab repository, checks it out into your personal user namespace, and registers an edit claim for every artifact you open. Embedded Git exports each artifact to a file on save, so no manual export step exists.
